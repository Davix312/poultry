Poultry Farming Tips and Advice

Welcome to the PoultryLink Uganda Poultry Tips page. Here, farmers can learn practical ways to care for their birds, improve production, prevent diseases and manage poultry farms more effectively.

Poultry Farming Tips

1. Provide Proper Housing

Build a poultry house that protects birds from rain, strong sunlight, predators and extreme temperatures. Ensure that the house has enough space and good ventilation.

Keep the floor litter dry and clean. Wet litter can encourage disease and make birds uncomfortable.

2. Provide Quality Feed

Give birds balanced poultry feed suitable for their age and purpose. Chicks, broilers, growers and laying hens have different nutritional requirements.

Buy feed from reliable suppliers and store it in a clean, dry place to protect it from moisture, pests and mould.

3. Supply Clean Water

Ensure that birds have access to clean water throughout the day. Clean drinkers regularly and refill them when necessary.

Dirty or insufficient water can affect feed intake, growth and egg production.

4. Prevent Poultry Diseases

Keep poultry houses clean and restrict unnecessary visitors. Clean and disinfect equipment, and avoid sharing dirty tools between poultry farms.

Consult a qualified veterinary professional to establish an appropriate vaccination and disease-prevention plan.

5. Observe Your Birds Daily

Check whether birds are eating, drinking and moving normally. Look for unusual droppings, breathing problems, weakness or unexpected deaths.

Seek veterinary advice promptly when birds appear sick. Separate affected birds where appropriate and follow professional guidance to reduce the risk of spreading disease.

6. Keep Farm Records

Record the number of birds, feed purchases, medication and vaccination information, deaths, eggs collected and sales made.

These records help farmers monitor performance, estimate costs and identify whether the poultry business is making a profit.

7. Manage Eggs Carefully

Collect eggs regularly using clean hands or suitable equipment. Handle them carefully to reduce breakages and contamination.

Keep eggs in a clean storage area and follow appropriate food-safety practices when preparing them for sale.

8. Plan Your Poultry Business

Before buying birds, estimate the cost of housing, chicks, feed, water, veterinary care and labour. Identify potential customers and suppliers.

Start with a flock that you can manage properly and expand when your resources, experience and market demand can support growth.

Important Advice for Poultry Farmers

Poultry management practices vary according to bird type, age, production system and local disease risks. Do not administer medicines or vaccines without appropriate professional guidance. Contact a qualified veterinary professional when you suspect a disease outbreak.
